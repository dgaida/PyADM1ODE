    Influence of the Values¶

    Every value of a substrate file acts on one of four quantities of the simulation: how much gas forms, how fast it forms, how much nitrogen reaches the digester, and how well the digester is buffered. This page first shows which value acts on what, then a worked example with numbers, and finally the formulas behind it.

    Which value acts on what¶

    Value Acts on Direction
    TS Gas amount, nitrogen load, loading rate More dry matter per tonne brings proportionally more of everything: gas, nitrogen, organic load.
    VS Gas amount More organic matter in the dry matter, correspondingly less ash.
    BMP (or D_VS, aXI) Gas amount Sets which share of the organic matter is degradable. The rest leaves the digester as inert material.
    NDF Speed More cell wall moves degradable material from the fast to the slow pool. At a fixed BMP the potential stays the same, but in the digester a larger part is washed out before it is degraded.
    ADL Almost nothing while BMP is given Lignin is inert. At a fixed BMP the degradation level is fitted so that the potential matches; ADL then only shifts the make-up of the inert rest. With a fixed D_VS, ADL limits how much of the cell wall is degradable.
    RP Nitrogen, pH, inhibition Protein releases ammonium when degraded. More protein raises ammonium and pH and can inhibit methane formation.
    RL Methane content Lipids yield more methane per kilogram and a gas richer in methane than carbohydrates.
    NDICP Speed of protein degradation Protein bound in the cell wall is only released with the cell wall and goes to the slow pool.
    NH4 / Snh4 Nitrogen, pH, inhibition Ammonium the substrate already carries. Acts like RP, but at once.
    KS43 / TAC Buffer Bicarbonate of the substrate. Together with ammonium and acids it sets the charge balance of the influent.
    Sac, Spro, Sbu, Sva (or FFS) Acid load Acids already fermented. They are available at once and burden the buffer; propionate and butyrate first have to be converted to acetate.
    pH Charge balance of the influent Sets which share of the acids and the ammonium is dissociated, and with it the cations and anions the substrate brings along.
    Temp Heat demand No influence on the biology (the digester has its own temperature), but on the heating: every kelvin colder costs about 1.2 kWh of heat per tonne of substrate.
    cost Costs Only enters the cost calculation.
    substrate_class Defaults A slurry or manure class assigns protein and lipids without a measurement to the slow pool, otherwise to the fast one.

    Worked example¶

    A digester of 3000 m³ at 40 °C receives 30 t of maize silage (maize_silage_milk_ripeness) and 10 t of cattle slurry (cattle_manure) per day, with the default kinetics. In each row one value of one file is changed, everything else stays the same. Shown is the steady state after 400 days.     What the table shows:

    • The gas amount hangs on TS and BMP. Both act almost proportionally. They are the values to measure first for a real plant.
    • NDF sets the sluggishness. Between 40 and 65 % cell wall the half-time lies between 4.1 and 6.0 days; the gas amount changes by a few percent only.
    • Nitrogen decides on stability. More protein or ammonium raises the ammonium in the digester, and the acids rise. At 10 % crude protein in the maize the digester tips over (11 g/l acids).
    • The storage state of the silage is secondary. pH and acetic acid of the silage change the gas amount by less than 0.5 %.
    • More buffer in the substrate does not help here. More bicarbonate in the slurry raises the pH, with it the share of free ammonia, and the acids rise. This holds only because the base case already sits close to ammonia inhibition.

    The model is sensitive to nitrogen

    With the default kinetics the base case already sits close to ammonia inhibition. Protein-rich rations (grass silage, poultry manure, much slurry) lead to high acid levels faster in the model than in practice, where the biology adapts to ammonia. If you compute such rations, calibrate the inhibition constant K_I_nh3 against plant data.

    The numbers hold for this one operating point. At another loading rate or retention time the magnitudes change, the directions remain.

    How the measured values are used¶

    Cell wall instead of crude fibre. With NDF and ADL the carbohydrates are split by the cell wall:

    slow pool   X_PS_ch = d · (NDF − ADL)                 structural carbohydrates
fast pool   X_PF_ch = VS − RP − RL − NDF              non-fibre carbohydrates
inert       X_I     = ADL + (1 − d) · (NDF − ADL)     lignin and non-degradable fibre
            d       = (NDF − VS · (1 − D_VS)) / (NDF − ADL)

    The non-degradable volatile solids VS · (1 − D_VS) are assigned to the cell wall, lignin first (Koch et al. 2010). Protein and lipids count as degradable. d is limited to 0…1: non-degradable matter beyond the whole cell wall is spread over the cell contents, and lignin stays inert even if D_VS claims more. Each fraction is converted to COD with its own factor, lignin with 1.563 kgCOD/kg. The total COD equals the COD of the composition, and the degraded share of the volatile solids equals D_VS.

    Inert share from a measured methane potential. With BMP and neither D_VS nor aXI, the degradability is fitted until the model potential equals the measured one:

    model potential = degradable COD · 0.350 Nm³ CH4/kgCOD / VS  =  BMP / BMP_recovery

    With a cell-wall analysis the degradation level D_VS is fitted, otherwise the inert share aXI. Both change the potential monotonically, so the result is unique. A potential the composition cannot reach raises an error. The same applies to a file of the standard schema that gives BMP but no aXI; in code, fit_degradability_to_bmp(params) does the fit.

    BMP_recovery defaults to 1: the model potential is matched to the measured one directly. Two effects of opposite sign are left out by that. A batch test recovers about 5 % less methane than the degradable COD holds, because biomass grows (Weißbach 2009). The model counts carbohydrates as glucose, which is about 10 % below the COD of starch and cellulose. Setting BMP_recovery: 0.95 accounts for the first effect alone; for the cereal whole-plant silage and the onions the composition then no longer reaches the standard yield.

    Protein bound in the cell wall. NDICP (neutral detergent insoluble crude protein, Licitra et al. 1996) is the protein that stays in the NDF residue. It is released only as the cell wall disintegrates and enters the slow pool; the remaining protein enters the fast pool:

    slow pool   X_PS_pr = NDICP                     X_PS_ch = d · (NDF − NDICP − ADL)
fast pool   X_PF_pr = RP − NDICP                X_PF_ch = VS − RP − RL − (NDF − NDICP)

    The bound protein is weighed with the NDF, so it is taken out of the structural carbohydrates; this is the non-fibre carbohydrate formula of NRC (2001). fsOTS / ffOTS then only split the lipids. Lipids have no such measurement: the detergent removes them, they belong to the cell contents.

    Single acids. Each acid enters its own ADM1 state. Propionate and butyrate then pass acetogenesis, which FFS (all acetate) skips.

    Titrated acid capacity. A titration to pH 4.3 also protonates acid anions. They are subtracted, so only the rest counts as bicarbonate:

    S_hco3 = TAC − Σ S_acid · (α(pH) − α(4.3))        limited to ≥ 0

    For the swine manure of the Gummersbach plant this leaves 51 instead of 212 mol/m³. KS43 of the standard schema is still taken as bicarbonate directly.

    Filtrate COD. COD_S beyond the acids and the soluble inerts is matter that is already disintegrated. It enters the hydrolysable pools X_S_* and is taken out of the disintegration pools in proportion.

    Biomass. X_bac and X_meth are split over the seven ADM1 groups in the ratios of the digester inoculum and taken out of the disintegration pools. load_gummersbach_library() adds the biomass of the legacy model to manure classes (0.25 and 0.0003 kgCOD/m³ at a storage age of 10 days); manure_age=None switches it off.

    Remuneration class. substrate_class is free text and is not used by the model. The library uses the classes of the German EEG 2012 as listed in the BiomasseV: EK I for energy crops (Anlage 2), EK II for manure (Anlage 3), EK 0 for substrates without a substrate-related tariff (Anlage 1). A financial module can key tariffs, the maize cap or greenhouse-gas factors to it. The field is available in both schemas and is stored in SubstrateParams.

    What is still not measured¶

    • The split of the lipids between the slow and the fast pool, and of the
      protein without NDICP (fsOTS / ffOTS). Default: slow for manure classes, fast otherwise. In the library only maize silage and corn-cob mix carry an NDICP; no sourced value was found for the other substrates.
    • The disintegration rates. The legacy library holds a rate kdis per
      substrate; ADM1da has two pools with fixed rates that are calibrated per digester. kdis, khyd_* and km_* are read and ignored.

    Differences to the legacy model¶

    • One disintegration pool with a rate per substrate becomes two pools, split
      by the cell wall.
    • The legacy model subtracts all acid anions from TAC, not only the titrated
      share.
    • Density uses additive volumes instead of the mass-weighted mean.
    • cost of the legacy library is in EUR/m³ and is converted to EUR/t FM.

    Quality of the legacy values

    Several values of the legacy library are not measurements of the substrate they describe. NDF / ADF / ADL of maize are literature values; green rye, onions, corn-cob mix and solid cattle manure carry the same NDF without a source. The degradation level of swine manure (0.95) has no source. COD_S is labelled as an assumption for five substrates and has no source for the other four. The library files under data/substrates/ no longer use these values; gummersbach:<id> still does.
